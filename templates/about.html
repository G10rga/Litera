About Litera

Georgian schoolchildren are asked to read texts written in a language nine centuries away from the one they speak. The usual remedy is a summary that replaces the text. Litera takes the opposite approach: keep the original, put a modern rendering next to it, and explain the hard words where they occur.

What is here

  • ვეფხისტყაოსანი, chapter by chapter, with a modern-Georgian rendering and positional glosses.
  • შუშანიკის წამება, in twenty sections, with a glossary applied by word match.
  • A growing library of shorter works, each with its provenance recorded.
  • The collected aphorisms, numbered for citation.

Where the texts come from

Original texts are public-domain transcriptions, imported from published digital editions and stored with their source URL, revision and licence. Each reader page prints that provenance at the bottom, so you can check any line against the edition it came from.

Modern renderings and glosses are marked draft, reviewed or final in the database, and the reader shows that status rather than hiding it. A draft is a draft.

What is not here

No exam-question bank, no essay templates, no character-analysis pages, no AI assistant. Earlier versions of this site advertised all four. None of them existed, so the pages were removed. When something is built, it will appear on the site working.

Who makes it

One person, in their own time, with no funding and nothing for sale. The code is open: github.com/G10rga/Litera. Corrections to a transcription or a gloss are the most useful thing you can send.
